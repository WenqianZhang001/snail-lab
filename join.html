Join us

Build sensing systems with us

The lab is new and growing. If you want to work on problems that span signal processing, hardware, learning, and real robots — and you want to see your system run outside a simulator — we would like to hear from you.

Prospective PhD students

Apply directly to the Yale graduate program in Electrical Engineering or Computer Science and name the SNAIL Lab and Prof. Boroushaki in your statement of purpose. Admissions are handled by the department committee, so an application is the only way to be considered — but feel free to send a short note with your CV so we can look out for it.

Current Yale students

Masters and undergraduate students are welcome to join for research projects, senior theses, and independent study. Email the PI with your CV, a transcript, and a paragraph on which of our research directions interests you and why.

Postdoctoral researchers

We consider postdoctoral applications on a rolling basis. Send your CV, two representative papers, and a short research statement describing what you would want to build here.

Visitors and collaborators

We host visiting students and researchers, and we collaborate with groups in wireless systems, robotics, and HCI. Get in touch with a description of the project you have in mind and your intended dates.

What we look for

Backgrounds that fit

No single background is required, and nobody arrives knowing all of this. We care more about curiosity, persistence with hardware, and a willingness to learn across areas.

  • Wireless communication and signal processing
  • Computer vision, machine learning, and robot learning
  • Robotic manipulation, control, and systems integration
  • Embedded systems, RF hardware, and antenna design
  • Human-computer interaction, AR/VR, and user studies
Before you email

Common questions

Should I email before applying to the PhD program?

You are welcome to, and a short, specific note is always read. Please include your CV and one or two sentences about which of our projects you would want to work on. Because of volume we cannot promise an individual reply, and an email is never a substitute for a formal application.

Do I need a wireless or RF background?

No. Our projects need people who are strong in at least one of sensing, robotics, learning, or systems, and who are willing to learn enough of the others to make a full system work.

Are there funded positions?

Admitted PhD students in the department are funded. Funding for postdocs and visitors depends on current grants — ask, and we will tell you what is available.

Can undergraduates do real research here?

Yes. Undergraduate researchers in the lab build and evaluate parts of real systems, and strong contributions lead to co-authorship.
